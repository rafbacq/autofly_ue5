# Run 6 (2026-10-06): a static pillar boundary, a wider mover margin and a V-shaped altitude cost

Run 5 gated 0.885 against the 0.95 bar (`docs/gates/m2d_r5_gate.json`; `2026-10-05-s01d-r5-margins.md`). The user
then asked for anything that would keep improving the expert's accuracy, run 6 included if it would help.

## What run 5's failures showed

**More hours of run 5 would not get there.** Its training success, stochastic and under its training rules, rose to
0.70 by 320k and then sat at 0.65–0.70 to the end at 561k.

**Its failures are near misses.** Replaying 15 gate episodes (`runs/viz/s01d_r5_final/`, same checkpoint, scene
config and observation as the gate) changed 8 outcomes:
- none of the 3 static collisions happened again;
- 2 of the 3 mover collisions did not happen again;
- 2 gate successes failed.

So physics noise decides flights that pass obstacles too closely. Measured on those replays
(`runs/m2d_diag/clearances.py`):

| What | Measured |
|---|---|
| Static pillars | Gate collisions ended 0.52–0.63 m from the surface (contact). 7 of 15 replays passed within 0.8 m. |
| Movers | Successes passed at 1.28–1.50 m, at the 1.3 m training boundary; gate contacts at 0.97–0.99 m. |
| Altitude | Both exits cruised at 1.5–1.8 m (the penalty zone's edge), then lost 0.8 m in 9 steps. |

**The pattern.** Every expert flies to the boundary it trains on, and its deterministic tail reaches about 0.3 m
inside it:
- For movers, run 5 moved that boundary out by 0.3 m, and gate mover collisions fell from 35 to 7.
- That margin barely cost training success: 0.63–0.70 late in run 5, against 0.61–0.68 for run 4 without it.
- For static pillars the boundary was still physical contact.
- For altitude it was the edge of the penalty zone.

## The changes

All three are training-only. The expert observes, and the gate scores, the task's own rules: physical contact, the
1.0 m mover rule, and the 1–3 m band.

1. **`--static-contact-m 1.1`** (new code, `expert/static_contact.py`, 3a0c3b7).
   - A step whose path passes within 1.1 m of a static pillar's surface ends the episode as a collision. That is
     0.5 m outside physical contact.
   - Every pair of pillars is at least 3.41 m apart surface to surface, so no passage closes: the narrowest keeps
     1.2 m.
   - Starts keep 1.4 m from every pillar, and the trainer refuses a boundary of 1.4 m or more.
2. **`--mover-contact-margin 0.5`** (run 5: 0.3).
   - A mover contact ends a training episode at 1.5 m, 0.5 m outside the 1.0 m rule.
   - Movers yield at 2.5 m, so 1 m of room remains in front of a yielding mover.
3. **`--altitude-margin-penalty 0.2 1.0`** (run 5: 0.1 0.5). The cost is now a V shape:
   - 0 at 2.0 m, 0.1 per step at 1.5 or 2.5 m, and 0.2 at the band's edges.
   - Run 5's cost was flat across 1.5–2.5 m. The policy cruised at the zone's edge and gave the vertical action no
     reason to hold a height, which is where its deterministic drift came from.
   - For scale: progress pays about 0.35 per step, so cruising at 1.8 m costs 0.04 per step.
   - The policy sees its altitude (`dz` in the state vector).

**Unchanged from run 5:** 4 workers, 12 h, a 250k buffer, the seeds, and the mover input. Also
`--mover-clearance-penalty 0.5 1.0`; it is measured from the 1.0 m rule, so now it charges only between 1.5 and 2.0 m.

**The aim:** about 0.5 m between where the expert trains to stop and where the task fails, for each kind of failure.
Run 5 had 0.3 m against movers and none against static pillars.

## What to watch

**Training numbers read differently again:**
- `outcomes/collision_static_margin` counts contacts at 1.1 m that the task would not score. Expect it high early, then
  falling.
- Mover collisions count at 1.5 m.
- Training success will run lower than run 5's at equal steps, because the rules are stricter. On its own that is not
  a failure signal.
- The eval watch, selection and the gate use the task's rules, so they compare directly with runs 1–5.

**Risks:**
1. Stricter rules slow learning. Compare the eval watch with run 5's at equal steps, not the training success.
2. Caution could cause timeouts (the step limit is 300).
3. SAC's fixed entropy target could push exploration noise out of the vertical channel and into yaw or speed.

**Afterwards:** two-stage selection, the deterministic gate, the audit, and a paired comparison with run 5 on the
gate's 200 episodes. The bar stays 0.95; a failed gate stays failed.

## Weight averaging

The selection showed the deterministic policy varying from one checkpoint to the next more than it improved along the
run. So I averaged checkpoints' weights (`scripts/average_checkpoints.py`). The network has no running statistics, so
the mean of the parameters is the whole model.

**Comparison.** Three means flew the same 100 *fresh* validation episodes (seed base 500002000, never used before),
beside the stage-2 winner. The rule, set before any result: gate the best mean only if it clearly beats the winner.

| Model | Success | Exits | Mover contacts |
|---|---|---|---|
| step_240000 | 0.83 | 9 | 6 |
| mean of 500k–550k (6) | 0.88 | 9 | 2 |
| mean of 400k–550k (16) | 0.91 | 8 | 1 |
| **mean of 250k–550k (31)** | **0.94** | **0** | 5 |

The wider the window, the fewer the exits. Against the winner, the 31-checkpoint mean won 17 episodes and lost 6
(McNemar p = 0.035).

**Its gate** (`m2d_r6_swa_gate.json`, sources and hashes in `m2d_r6_swa_sources.json`):
- **187/200 = 0.935** (CI 0.892–0.962). **It fails by three episodes.** Audit: 0 flagged.
- Failures: 8 mover contacts, 2 above the ceiling, 2 lateral, 1 static.
- On the same 200 episodes:
  - against run 1: 42 won, 10 lost (p = 9.1e-06);
  - against run 5: 22 won, 12 lost (p = 0.12);
  - against step_240000: 14 won, 11 lost (p = 0.69).

This is run 6's second gate on these episodes. The mean was chosen on fresh validation episodes before its gate, and
both results stand.

## What is left

**Mover contacts are the largest failure: 8 of the mean's 13.**
- Every one was a near miss, 0.90–1.00 m from the surface.
- Almost all came at full speed (1.7–2.0 m/s), passing a mover that had stopped to yield, at 28–73° off the heading.
- A wider training boundary did not move them: 7 at 1.3 m (run 5), 5 and 8 at 1.5 m (run 6). So distance is not the
  lever. Speed near a mover is the next candidate:
  - a training boundary that grows with the drone's speed, or
  - a cost on speed close to a mover.

**Weight averaging should stay.** It turned exits from the commonest deterministic failure into a rare one.

## The lateral exits are systematic

All three lateral exits in the mean's render repeated on replay (episodes 12, 97 and 92). The cause is a straight route
that runs close to the boundary. The sampler never puts the start and the target on the same edge (0 of 200 gate
episodes), but they can sit near the same corner, on adjacent edges.

Only 2 of the 200 gate episodes have a route that passes within 2 m of the edge (the closest approach, stopping 5 m
short of the target): 12 at 1.0 m and 184 at 1.7 m. Both produced lateral exits, 12 for the mean and 184 for run 5.
The third, 97, passes at 2.5 m, about the 15th percentile. So this concerns roughly 1-1.5% of episodes, a secondary
lever.

Targets sit a median 1.3 m from the edge, and the success radius is 5 m. So a lateral training margin could keep the
drone off the boundary without making any target unreachable. It would be new code, and a candidate for a later run.
