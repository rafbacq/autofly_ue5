# 2026-10-03 — s01d run 2: what changes, and why

The user asked me to keep improving the results and to choose the levers myself. M2d's gate failed: best_model scored
0.775 and final 0.535 against 0.95 (`docs/decisions/2026-10-03-m2d-closeout.md`). Run 2 (`runs/expert/s01d_r2`)
changes four things, each aimed at a measured failure.

## 1. Leaving the bounds costs as much as a collision (b506a5f)

Spec §8 priced a collision at 10 and leaving the bounds at 5. `final` learned the cheaper ending: when a collision
looked likely it dove out of the altitude band (58 of its 62 out-of-bounds gate episodes, `altitude_low`, at a median
of 79 steps). `best_model`'s 10 `altitude_high` exits look like the same escape upward. s01d's scene file now sets
`r_bounds = 10`. Static s01 keeps M2's reward exactly (the golden test passes); the run identity and the gate record
carry the effective coefficients.

## 2. The expert is told where the nearby movers are (ea15141)

A mover's contact rule fires 1.0 m from its surface; a static pillar needs physical contact (about 0.48 m). A mover
within 2.5 m of the drone yields: it stands still, and then looks exactly like a static pillar in depth. In RGB it
does too, since both are white. So a depth-only expert cannot know which pillars need the larger clearance. Of
`best_model`'s 35 mover collisions, 19 came with the mover out of the forward view: passing beside it, too close.

The observation gains a `movers` key. It holds the 4 nearest movers whose surface is within 10 m, nearest clearance
first. Each mover has 6 values in the body frame:
- present;
- forward and right offset;
- clearance to the contact boundary;
- forward and right velocity over the last step, zero while it yields.

It goes into an optional extractor branch. Every earlier checkpoint loads and acts unchanged.

The expert is the data source, not the student. The dataset records RGB and state[9] whatever the expert observes,
so this costs the dataset nothing.

**Measured before any change** (`docs/gates/m2d_r1_contact_probe_{0,1}.json`): r1's best_model was flown on 150 s01d
episodes from the probe range (300.7e6 and 300.8e6, not the gate's), with the new per-contact details. It succeeded on
0.827. All 24 of its mover collisions had the drone **0.84–1.00 m from the mover's surface** (median 0.94), so none
was physical. 21 of them were at 45–90° off the heading, 19 had the mover standing still (yielding), and the drone
was at full speed (median 1.95 m/s). The expert passes beside a stationary mover at a clearance that is fine for a
static pillar and breaks the mover rule: exactly what the mover input reports.

## 3. Warm start from s01d_r1's best_model (b0b12ff)

s01d_r1's best_model already flies the field at 0.775. Run 2 copies every parameter. The extractors' heads gain zero
columns for the new branch, so at step 0 the network acts and values exactly like the source (tested to 1e-5).

The warm-up acts with that policy instead of SB3's uniform random actions. The entropy coefficient comes across as
well. The replay buffer starts empty: the old one lacks the mover input.

## 4. The checkpoint is chosen on validation episodes (2299b3a)

Training-time evaluation (20 episodes) picked r1's best_model at 0.85; the gate measured 0.775. Run 2 scores its
checkpoints on held-out validation seeds (`SELECTION_SEED_BASE`, 500e6) with the gate's own machinery, in parallel on
four slots, and re-scores the leaders on further validation episodes. Only the winner, and `final` for the record, are
gated on the gate's seeds.

## Kept as before

- SAC's hyperparameters (spec §8) and the 3-frame float16 depth stack.
- The 1 ms config, 4 workers, and the 12 h budget (resumable).
- The gate: 200 episodes, 0.95 deterministic.
- Training-time evaluation runs every 50k steps instead of every 25k. Each evaluation stops training for about ten
  minutes, and selection no longer depends on it.

## What would show it worked

In training: fewer mover collisions without the out-of-bounds rate rising, and evaluations at or above r1's 0.85.
At the gate: ≥ 0.95. If it falls short, the mover-contact details now recorded per episode (gap, bearing, moving or
yielding) will say why.
